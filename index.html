The 15-second open

Say this before anything else

Four beats. It fits in fifteen seconds, it is all defensible, and it hands the listener the three things that make ReLEAF unusual.

“ReLEAF makes the plant protectant on the farm, on the day the crop needs it. The engineered cells never leave the loop — a 0.2 µm membrane keeps them in and lets the protein out. We ran it for 102 hours and watched the protein cross while the cells did not.”

      Guard rails

      Never claim, always have

      Honesty beats polish. The pages disagree in places and they say so — naming it first is the strongest move available at the booth.

      Do not claim

      A judge will test any of these in two clicks
      • A working light switch. It is modulation, not switching: supported at 13 h, unresolved at 16 h, n = 2.
      • A validated field effect. No protectant has been through a field trial.
      • A titre. 2.5–6.5 µg/mL is low for a bioprocess, and longer runs do not mean bigger harvests.
      • A price the pages agree on. US$2,280 vs US$3,800 per hectare, and they name each other.
      • “Purified” protein. Everything applied was 0.22 µm filtered crude supernatant.
      • “First”. Say rare in high-school iGEM and give the evidence instead.
      • That we proved AmilCP failed on folding. We reasoned to it; the control was never run.

      Three sentences for every pocket

      They work on almost any hard question
      • “Our statistics are on the instrument, not on the biology — that is the next experiment, and we know which one.”
      • “That result goes against us, and here it is.”
      • “The pages disagree and they say so — here is which one is the authority.”
      And the one they always ask
      • “Name the two links never demonstrated”: forecast → light (no prediction has ever driven a lamp onto a culture) and permeate → irrigation (every plant experiment was applied by hand).
